# License

## In one sentence

OpenDocBot is a **fair-code** project: the source code and development are public on GitHub so you
can fully audit it, it is free for individuals and teams of up to **30 people**, and
organizations that scale past that threshold need a commercial license.

## What you can do

* **View and audit the source.** The entire codebase is public on
  [GitHub](https://github.com/opendocbot/opendocbot). You can read every line and
  verify exactly what the add-in does with your data.
* **Use it.** Run it locally, in Word, Excel and PowerPoint, for free.
* **Modify it.** Change the code and build your own version.
* **Fork it.** Take the project in your own direction. The license notice must
  stay intact, and you cannot present your fork as the official OpenDocBot.
* **Self-host it.** Deploy it on your own infrastructure, even fully airgapped,
  with no connection to us.
* **Deploy it inside an organization of up to 30 users.** No license required.

## What requires a commercial license

* **Enterprise deployments beyond 30 users.** Once more than 30 people in an
  organization use the add-in, a commercial license is required. This is the
  line that funds the project's development.
* **Removing or replacing the OpenDocBot branding.** You cannot strip the
  OpenDocBot name and logo and present the software as your own, unless you have
  a written enterprise agreement.

## Why this model

OpenDocBot is designed to be as open as possible without giving away financial sustainability. 
The free tier up to 30 users covers individuals, small teams,
and evaluation. Organizations large enough to pay are asked to pay, which keeps
the project maintained without telemetry, lock-in, or hidden fees. 

Exceptions to these restrictions 
may be granted at our sole discretion for educational institutions, academic research, 
non-profit organizations, and recognized open-source projects. 
Reach out to `hello@opendocbot.com` to apply for a community grant.

Everything that makes OpenDocBot trustworthy is in the open and verifiable:
zero telemetry, 100% client-side processing, and your data never reching our servers.

## What to call it

This model is commonly called **fair-code** or **source-available**. It is not
"open source" in the strict sense of the Open Source Initiative, because the
usage limits and branding protection go beyond what OSI-approved licenses allow.
The difference rarely matters in practice: the code is fully public, and the
restrictions only kick in at the enterprise tier.
